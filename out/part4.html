Tip: turn a pasted brief into a tracker row

Paste a campaign brief into any AI chat tool and ask: "Pull out the goal, audience, deadline, channels and the three messages this brief stresses most. Give them to me as one row I can paste into a spreadsheet."

How you might use it: it saves the copy-and-paste step and keeps every campaign in one place, in any spreadsheet or notes app. Read the row before relying on it, since an AI can misread a brief.

WORTH A LOOK

The Microsoft 365 connector for Claude

It lets Claude search and read files in OneDrive and SharePoint, and work with Outlook and Teams, according to Claude's Help Center. For marketing teams that keep briefs, decks and reports in Microsoft's tools, it means Claude can work with those files directly.

The catch: a Microsoft Entra Global Administrator has to approve the integration for the whole organization before anyone can connect, per the setup page. So a work account may need an admin's yes first, and a plain personal account may not have that option. Check with your IT team before you spend time on it. No admin approval? Download the file and upload it to any AI chat tool instead. We did not connect or test anything.

A first request, once connected: "Find the most recent campaign brief in OneDrive and list three places it could be clearer." Check the tool's own setup page for steps.

Anthropic's prompting guide compares Claude to a brilliant new employee who lacks your context, so the clearer you are, the better the result. The "ask questions first" move below is our own way of applying that idea: let the AI find the missing context before it writes anything.

WEAK

Write me a LinkedIn post about our product launch.

STRONG. COPY AND PASTE THIS

I want help with this task: [what you want, for example: a LinkedIn post announcing a product launch].

Before you write anything, ask me up to 5 questions about the things you would need to do this well, such as audience, goal, tone and facts. Ask them all at once, numbered, and keep each one short.

Wait for my answers. Do not start the draft yet. If I answer "skip" to a question, tell me what you will assume instead.

After I answer, write the first version. Then list any assumptions you made.

Why the strong one works

  • Gaps get found early. The AI names what it does not know before it guesses.

  • You stay in charge. A cap of 5 questions keeps it short, and you decide which details matter.

  • No wasted draft. "Do not start yet" stops the AI from writing a generic version you would only throw away.

  • Assumptions show. The closing list tells you what the AI filled in on its own, so you can fix it.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY. 5 MINUTES

Use it on something real, such as a campaign brief, a social post or a content calendar. Answer the questions in one short message, then compare the result with what a one-line request would have given you. About 5 minutes.
