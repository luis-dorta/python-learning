Tip: turn a content calendar into a to-do list

Paste your content calendar into any AI chat tool and ask: "Count how many posts are planned this week, list any with no owner or no due date, and draft a one-line reminder for each. Show me the list before you change anything."

How you might use it: it turns a busy calendar into a short to-do list. Asking to see the list first lets you catch mistakes before you rely on it. Remove names and private details before you paste.

Before you ask for a draft, write the rules a good answer must follow. This is a general prompting habit that follows Anthropic's advice to be clear and direct. The exact layout below is our own version.

WEAK

Write a campaign brief for this product launch.

STRONG. COPY AND PASTE THIS

Write a one-page campaign brief for the product below.

Product: [paste a short description]

About the campaign: [3 short bullets: the audience, the main goal, the budget or timing]

A good brief will:

1. Stay under 250 words.

2. Open with one specific reason this audience should care, not "We are excited to announce."

3. Connect two of my bullets to two concrete actions for the team.

4. Sound confident: plain words, no buzzwords.

5. End with one clear next step.

If any rule cannot be met with what I gave you, tell me what is missing instead of making something up.

Why the strong one works

  • The target is clear. The AI knows what "good" means before it writes, so it stops guessing.

  • Easy to judge. You can check the draft against five rules instead of going by feel.

  • Specific, not generic. Rules that tie your bullets to real actions push out empty filler.

  • Gaps get flagged. The last line asks for what is missing, so it will not invent details.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY. 5 MINUTES

Pick one real product or campaign. Run the prompt with your three bullets, then check the draft against each rule. Change one rule, for example the word limit, and run it again to see what shifts. About 5 minutes.

Sources: Anthropic prompting best practices (general advice on being clear and direct). We did not find one page that names this exact technique, so the rule list format is our own adaptation.
