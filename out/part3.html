STORY 2 | OPENAI

ChatGPT Wallet: an AI agent can buy things, with your approval

OpenAI. On Friday, November 13, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet for its ChatGPT assistant. You add a card once. When an agent in ChatGPT Work, Codex or dots wants to buy something, you see the merchant, items, total and card, then approve with a security check such as Face ID. OpenAI says the store receives a payment token, not your real card number, and that your card's rewards and dispute protections stay in place. It was built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa.

The limits matter. OpenAI's help page says it is for ChatGPT Pro users who are 18 or older and live in the U.S. It supports eligible U.S.-issued American Express, Chase, Mastercard and Visa credit cards. Debit cards and virtual cards are not supported. OpenAI's launch post says it is initially available through dots, and that over time you will be able to pre-approve purchases within limits you set. That last part is a plan, not a feature today.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

The pattern to notice is "the human approves, the agent does the legwork." For marketing, an agent that compares products and checks out changes what a good product page needs: clear specs and prices an AI can read. It also shows why approval steps and clear records are becoming part of how AI products earn customer trust. That is our reading.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Pick a product your brand sells, or one you know well. Ask any AI chat tool to list the five facts it would need from a store page to recommend it with confidence. Then check whether the real product page shows all five.

Go deeper

  • OpenAI says a bank may ask you to verify the card at setup with a code or in its app. Chase cardholders must be the primary cardholder, not an authorized user, per the help page.

  • The approval screen also shows shipping and recurring-payment terms, per the help page.

  • The help page does not describe spending limits yet.

  • New Orleans CityBusiness also covered the launch. Most details here come from OpenAI's own pages.

  • No independent testing of the security claims yet. Treat them as OpenAI's design claims.

Sources: OpenAI (primary source, vendor-reported), OpenAI Help Center, New Orleans CityBusiness

STORY 3 | OAKLAND

Oakland's tentative city contract adds AI job protections

Oakland. On October 31, the City of Oakland announced a tentative three-year agreement with IFPTE Local 21, the union for many of its professional and technical workers. The city's release mentions "AI usage protections" but does not list the terms. It does give the money terms: a 9% raise over three years, in steps of 2.5%, 2.5% and 4%, plus transit benefits and pay adjustments for lower-paid jobs. The union dropped its strike notice for November 5.

The AI terms come from reporting. The Guardian, in a November 12 story on public workers pushing for AI safeguards, reports that the city would have to retrain or reassign workers displaced by AI. The deal is not final: the union holds a ratification vote, then the City Council votes. We did not find the contract text itself, so the exact wording is unconfirmed.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

This is one of the first concrete examples of AI rules written into a work contract. It covers public-sector workers, not private companies, so it does not apply to every job. Still, it shows that how AI changes roles is becoming something teams negotiate, not only a tool choice. Marketing teams rolling out AI tools will face the same question: which tasks change, and who decides. That is our read.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Write a three-line note a marketing team could share before adopting a new AI tool: what the tool will do, what stays with people, and who to ask with concerns. Then ask any AI chat tool what a team member might still worry about.

Go deeper

  • Under the city's release, final approval needs the full City Council. It says no pension or health care contributions were included.

  • The Guardian also describes other unions seeking AI terms, including a Transport Workers Union contract in Columbus, Ohio that gives the union veto power over autonomous vehicle plans.

  • Because the city page has no AI details, treat the retrain-or-reassign wording as secondhand until the contract is public.

Sources: City of Oakland (primary source), The Guardian

Apple's new study app answers health questions and studies how people use AI

Apple | Research study | Launched Nov 15

Apple and OpenEvidence, a healthcare AI company, launched an app that answers everyday medical questions with answers grounded in sources such as medical journals. Users can rate answers, take monthly surveys and, if they choose, share Apple Health data. Apple plans to run the study for more than 12 months, per TechCrunch.

Who can join: U.S. users over 21 with an iPhone 15 or later, iOS 26 or later and Apple Intelligence turned on. Privacy, as reported: data is anonymized before it goes to a secure cloud provider, is not used to train AI models, and the question and answer text is deleted after 24 hours.

Good for: seeing how a major brand tests AI trust in public. Do not treat it as medical advice.

Sources: TechCrunch (one outlet read, privacy details are Apple's as relayed)
