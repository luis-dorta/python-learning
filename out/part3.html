STORY 2 | MARKETS

Nvidia beats estimates, and its board says it does not intend to reinstate Huang

Markets. Yesterday's issue noted that Nvidia was about to report earnings. The results are now in. Nvidia reported on Tuesday, its first results since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last week. Per CNBC, adjusted earnings were $2.68 a share against $2.48 expected, and revenue was $115.84 billion against $109.10 billion expected, more than double the $57 billion of a year earlier. Data center revenue was $107.6 billion. The company guided to about $130 billion next quarter against $125.1 billion expected, and the stock rose more than 3% after hours.

On the leadership question, board chair Harvey Jones said on the call that the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." Interim CEO Colette Kress called it "business as usual." She also raised next year's growth outlook to 75% from 70%. On China, Nvidia said its outlook assumes no data center compute revenue from China. It also said major AI labs should make up about 15% of data center sales this year. Those are company statements, and the stock reaction can change by morning.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

Spending on AI infrastructure is still growing fast, even after a shock at the top. For marketers, that suggests AI vendors and AI features will keep competing for attention and budget. One quarter is not a trend, so treat this as one data point. That is our reading, not investment advice.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Write a three-sentence explanation of "beat expectations" for a coworker who does not follow stocks. Use the numbers above: what was expected, what happened, and why the gap matters. It is good practice for turning a dense report into a clear social post.

STORY 3 | ADVERTISING

LeBron James sues an AI betting app over an ad he says copied his likeness

Advertising. LeBron James filed a lawsuit in federal court in Manhattan on Tuesday against an AI sports betting app called Bron, its parent company PlayVision, and its two co-founders. His complaint says an AI-made ad showed a figure with his tattoos, his number 23 jersey and a simulated version of his voice scrolling through betting options. It says the ad drew about one million views on X in a day. He accuses the company of using the "Bron" name to suggest he endorsed the app, and he asks for damages and a court order stopping it.

According to CNBC, the startup took the ad down from X and Instagram after a cease-and-desist letter on November 5, and says it is working to change the app's name. The complaint argues the company knew publicity would outweigh legal risk. These are the plaintiff's allegations, and no court has ruled. Separately, a press-release survey commissioned by a research company says 43% of Americans have felt tricked by an AI ad, and more than half do not trust AI-advertised products to match their descriptions. We read an excerpt only of that release.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

For anyone making ads, the line is simple: do not copy a real person's face, voice or nickname without written permission. This case may become a reference point for how courts treat AI likeness in marketing. The survey is a sponsored company's own study, so use it as a hint about trust, not a fact about all consumers. That is our view, not legal advice.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Write four rules for how your team uses AI in marketing work. For example: no real people's likeness without permission, label AI-made images, keep a record of prompts, and check every claim. Save it as a short page anyone on the team can find.

Microsoft launches seven MAI models, with big cost claims

Microsoft | Announced Nov 17

At its Ignite event, Microsoft introduced seven of its own models. They cover coding, security, image editing, speech and routing. MAI Code 2 is for everyday company coding, and MAI Code 2 Flash is for small jobs like explaining code and quick fixes. MAI Image 2.7 handles image editing and generation, and MAI Realtime 1 is its first speech-to-speech model, starting at $0.01 per audio minute.

Where to get it: MAI Code 2 Flash is in GitHub Copilot and in limited preview in Microsoft Foundry. We could not confirm where the others are available. The catch: the comparisons, such as 98% of one rival's coding score at about 40% of the price, are Microsoft's own, relayed by one outlet. Good for: seeing that big software companies now build their own models, not only resell others. We have not tested any of them.

Sources: Interesting Engineering (one outlet, vendor-reported claims)

Tasks in Gemini is now free in six countries

Google | Gemini | Launched Nov 17

Google says its Gemini assistant can handle errands such as booking appointments, calling businesses and buying items, using a person's email, calendar and payment methods. It is free across the U.S., Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia and India, with the advanced features starting in the U.S. Users can set a spending limit upfront. Calling is not available in five U.S. states, including Indiana and Minnesota.

Good for: small errands. The catch: it needs access to personal accounts, so read the permissions first.

Sources: Google blog (primary source, vendor-reported)

Gemini Notebook can turn notes into a working site

Google | Notebook | Announced Nov 16

In Google's Gemini Notebook, type "make me a site" in a notebook's chat and describe what it should do. PPC Land says it can produce things like explainers, dashboards and trackers from the sources in that notebook. It is rolling out on the web for users 18 and older, including Workspace accounts, with mobile "coming soon." Price and limits are not stated.

Good for: quick one-page campaign mockups. Check every result, since Google warns it can make mistakes.

Sources: PPC Land (one outlet, Nov 17)
