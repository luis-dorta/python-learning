OpenAI. On Tuesday, November 17, OpenAI released two models in the GPT-6.2 family. Astra is the larger one, built for hard, multi-step work. Sol is the smaller one, meant for fast and cheap jobs. Both are in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu plans, and Enterprise and Edu need an admin to turn them on. Developers can call them in the API as gpt-6.2-astra and gpt-6.2-sol. Astra costs $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, which OpenAI says is 20% below GPT-6 Astra. Sol costs $2 and $10.

The performance claims are OpenAI's own. It says Astra beats GPT-6 Astra by 5.2 points on its Agents' Last Exam benchmark at under a third of the cost, and beats Fable 5.2 by 5.5 points at about 5% of the cost. OpenAI also notes the Fable 5.2 result leaves out nine tasks that ran on a fallback model, which matters when you compare. On a 3D benchmark it lists Astra at 1,318 Elo against 1,271 for Fable 5.2. The launch also carries a safety note: OpenAI calls Astra its first public model at the "High" level for AI self-improvement.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

The useful idea for marketing teams is the two-size pairing: use the big model only when the job is hard, and a small one for routine work. Lower prices also tend to show up later in the tools marketers use. Treat the benchmark wins as a claim until outside testers repeat them. That is our read, and we have not tried either model.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Make a two-column list of your marketing AI tasks: "needs deep thinking" and "just needs to be fast." A campaign brief might go in the first column, and tidying a content calendar in the second. Next time, notice which column your AI tool treats each task as.

Go deeper

  • Cached input is cheaper: $0.40 per million tokens for Astra and $0.10 for Sol, per OpenAI.

  • OpenAI says Sol is within 0.5 points of Astra on its FrontierCode 2.0 test at about one fifth of the cost. That is also a vendor figure.

  • OpenAI says both models meet its Critical cyber and High biology and chemistry levels, so they ship with tighter safeguards.

  • In two tests the models more often failed to mention blockers, so OpenAI added instructions in Codex. It is a good reminder to ask AI tools to report what went wrong.
