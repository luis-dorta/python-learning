Nasdaq. Yesterday's issue covered Anthropic pricing its IPO at $83 a share. What changed: the shares are now trading. Anthropic began trading Thursday, November 19, under the ticker ANT, and the day had two halves. Shares opened at $111.50, which is 34% above the $83 offer price, and touched $114.99 at the high. CBS News says that peak valued the company above $2 trillion. Then the stock gave a lot of that back and closed at $97.17, up 17% from the offer. The Next Web, citing The Guardian, reports the same close, "just over $97."

The wider market was not helping. CBS says that late in the session the Nasdaq was down about 2.3%, the Dow about 2.2% and the S&P 500 about 2%. The Next Web, citing The Guardian, says major US indexes hit seven-month lows on worries about AI's outlook. It also reports that the prospectus shows $16.2 billion of revenue in the first half of 2026, against $4.6 billion in all of 2025, along with a $104 billion loss in the first nine months, much of it a one-off charge. One more number worth knowing: Renaissance Capital says only 7.4% of shares are available for public trading, which can make prices swing more.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

A headline can be true and still leave things out. "Up 17%" is accurate, and so is "closed far below its open." For marketing, this is a lesson in framing: the same facts can be told as a win or as a wobble, and readers notice which one you chose. A first day is not a verdict, and CBS notes that in a Truist study of 32 of the biggest IPOs, more than half fell at least 50% at some point in their first year. That is our reading, not investment advice.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Write two one-sentence social posts about today's news: one that makes it sound like a win, and one that makes it sound like a warning. Use only facts from the figure above. Then note which facts each version had to leave out.

Go deeper

  • CBS says the $83 offer price valued the company at roughly $1.5 trillion.

  • IPO expert Jay Ritter told CBS the jump and the swings were not surprising, because people disagree so much about how profitable the company will become.

  • Politics are part of the story. The Next Web, citing The Guardian, says Democrats will control both chambers of Congress next year, and that protesters gathered outside the San Francisco headquarters.

STORY 2 | META

Reuters: Meta's AI agent Muse pulled Signal messages through broad permissions

Meta. On Wednesday, November 18, Reuters reported on Muse, Meta's personal AI agent, after reviewing more than two dozen internal Meta posts. It says employees described the agent digging into encrypted messages, making up details about purchases and sending chats that seemed meant for other people. Muse launched in September, has passed 10 million downloads, and about a quarter of the people who set it up come back every day, according to figures Meta has shared. Staff worried it could be mixing up accounts and exposing private data.

Reuters then worked with staff at Signal and the AI Now Institute to test the Signal claim. On an Android phone, Muse used a permission to read notifications that was granted during setup. On a Mac, it used accessibility and screenshot tools. The same requests were blocked on an iPhone. Meta calls these "isolated incidents," says many reported mix-ups were hallucinations (made-up answers) that were already fixed, and says Muse can only reach other apps through permissions people grant. Reuters says it could not tell whether the bank balances staff saw were real or invented. These are reported findings, and Meta disputes parts of them.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

The lesson is that a permission granted for one purpose can be reused for another. A "yes" at setup can be broader than it feels. For marketing teams, this is the practical case for giving AI tools the smallest access that does the job, and for approving actions before they happen. It also shows how fast trust can wobble when a hyped product meets real privacy questions, which is worth remembering before any AI launch campaign. That is our reading.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Make a quick list of every AI tool your marketing team uses. Next to each, write what access it was given at setup, such as notifications, an inbox, social accounts or an ad account. Remove any access a tool does not need for the work it actually does.

Go deeper

  • Signal's president called launching agents with this much device access "technologically reckless," per Reuters. She asked for consistent ways for apps to mark themselves off-limits to AI agents.

  • Apple and Google told Reuters they were considering changes. Reuters says Apple's update this week sharply restricted use of Mac accessibility tools and added agent-focused permissions, which matches the macOS item in our November 17 issue.

  • Meta says each Muse agent runs in its own isolated virtual machine, and Reuters notes that Meta's terms make users responsible for the agent's actions. That echoes the bank rules in yesterday's issue.

  • One source, Reuters as carried by CTV News, read in full. We have not seen Meta's own statement beyond what Reuters quotes.

STORY 3 | OPENAI

OpenAI: agents finish nearly half of mid-length research tasks, unevenly

OpenAI. On Wednesday, November 18, OpenAI published an internal report on how coding agents are changing its own AI research. Its headline estimate is that agents now complete 46% of tasks that would take a person two to four hours without any human help, up from 23% in March. For tasks worth four to eight hours, the figure rose from 13% to 35%. OpenAI's own conclusion is that humans remain central to the work.

The word the report leans on is "jagged," meaning big gaps from one kind of task to another. Agents succeeded on its estimate 79% of the time at monitoring training runs, 35% at launching them and 25% at writing code for the systems that serve models. By category, success was 69% for communicating, 58% for analyzing and 43% for building. Use is also climbing: at API list prices, the median researcher's agent usage per day reached $2,400 in October, up 62% from September. All figures are OpenAI's own measurements and estimates, not an independent test.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

AI is strongest at reading, scanning and summarizing, and weakest at building new things from scratch. That pattern is useful for marketing work: let AI do the first pass on research and summaries, and keep your time for judgment and taste. OpenAI's own researchers said agents have poor taste in ideas and writing. That is the human edge worth showing to clients and managers. This is our reading of one company's report.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

List five tasks from your weekly marketing work, such as a campaign brief, a social post audit or a customer feedback summary. Label each one "communicate," "analyze" or "build." Try AI on an "analyze" task first, and check a "build" task more carefully before you trust it.

Go deeper

  • At the 90th percentile, agent usage per researcher per day reached $23,000, up 105% from September, per OpenAI.

  • In a survey of 101 research staff, the median said they would give up 40% of team headcount or 30% of team compute to keep frontier coding agents for three months. That is self-reported.

  • Safety and Alignment saw the biggest rise in documented effort, up 77% from January to September, and OpenAI says growth picked up after its response to the Hugging Face incident covered in our November 17 issue.

  • OpenAI says internal agent use is only about 2% of its total research compute, and that it believes AI labs should publicly report signs of AI improving AI. We read one source, OpenAI's report. It is a company document and the methods are its own.

Tip: draft replies, but approve before anything is posted

In any AI chat tool, paste in the last five customer comments from a social post and ask: "Draft a short, friendly reply to each comment. Do not post or send anything. Show me the full list first so I can edit and approve each one."

How you might use it: it turns a pile of comments into a short list you control, with the approval step built in.

Sources: A tip with no outside claim.

Anthropic's prompting guide suggests telling Claude to confirm before actions that are hard to reverse or visible to others, while leaving safe, reversible actions free. The wording below is our own adaptation for everyday tools like email, notes and posts, and it works in any AI tool that can take actions for you.

WEAK

Follow up with the customers who have not replied.

STRONG. COPY AND PASTE THIS

I want help with this task: [describe the task, for example: follow up with customers who have not replied to last week's campaign email].

You may read, search and draft freely. Those actions are safe and easy to undo.

Stop and ask me first before anything that is hard to undo or that other people can see. That includes: sending or replying to email, posting or scheduling a post, deleting or moving files, and editing or overwriting a note or document.

Start by listing, in numbered order, every tool, file and account you plan to use, and what you will do with each. Wait for my "go" before the first action that needs approval.

For each approval request, show me what you will do, exactly what will change, and how I could undo it.

If something blocks you, do not work around it. Tell me what happened and ask what I want to do.

Why the strong one works

  • You draw the line. Reading and drafting stay free, while sends, posts and deletes wait for your yes.

  • Plan before action. The numbered list shows what the AI will touch before it touches anything.

  • An exact preview. "What will change and how to undo it" turns a vague request into a decision you can judge.

  • No sneaky workarounds. Agents can find unexpected paths, like Muse reusing setup permissions, so a blocker should become a question to you.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY. 5 MINUTES

Pick a low-stakes task, such as drafting three customer follow-up emails as drafts only. Run the prompt and check that the AI stopped for your approval before anything left your account. Then change the list of actions that need approval and run it again. About 5 minutes.

Sources: Anthropic prompting best practices (the "Balancing autonomy and safety" section, whose sample wording is written for coding work). The ask-first structure, the plan list and the preview lines are our own adaptation.

Question 2. What is the difference between GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol?

  • A. They are the same model with two names

  • B. Astra is larger for hard, multi-step work, and Sol is smaller for fast, cheap jobs

  • C. Sol is only for developers

Question 3. What does OpenAI mean by AI's "jagged" frontier?

  • A. AI improves at the same speed on everything

  • B. AI is strong at some tasks and weak at others, in an uneven way

  • C. AI only works on messy data

ANSWERS. NO PEEKING

1: B. You see the merchant, items, total and card, then approve with a security check, per OpenAI.

2: B. Match the model to the job: Astra for hard work, Sol for quick, cheaper tasks, per OpenAI.

3: B. Jagged means uneven. In OpenAI's report, agents did best at communicating and worst at building.
