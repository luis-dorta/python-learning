Markets. Our November 10 issue covered early reports on Anthropic's IPO. What changed: the price is now set. On Wednesday, November 18, Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range it had marked. It sold 980 million shares instead of the 825 million first planned, which brings in about $81.3 billion. TechCrunch reports that makes it the largest IPO ever by money raised, ahead of SpaceX's $75 billion. Trading starts today on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

At that price TechCrunch says Anthropic is worth roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis. That is well above the $965 billion from its May private round, and below the $2 trillion it was reportedly aiming for months ago. A group of big tech companies, including Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, also agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting shares in a separate private placement. The report says Amazon owns nearly 15% of the company and Google almost 14%. We could not check today's opening trades, so how the first day goes is still unknown.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

A pricing above range tells you investors wanted in, and a public Anthropic will now publish regular financial reports. That gives marketers real numbers to cite in pitches, reports and trend decks about where AI money goes. A price is not a prediction, so treat the first trading day as news, not proof. That is our reading, and it is not investment advice.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Write three sentences explaining "priced above range" to a friend who does not follow markets. Use the numbers: the planned range, the actual price, and why that gap signals demand. Save it as a draft social post and check it reads clearly without any jargon.

Go deeper

  • TechCrunch says none of Anthropic's venture investors holds more than 5%, and the seven co-founders each sold about 3.4 million shares, worth around $282 million each, per the S-1 filing.

  • The report ties some market nerves to the recent departure of Nvidia's CEO, which an earlier issue covered.

  • OpenAI was last reportedly valued below Anthropic and has not announced IPO plans, per TechCrunch. We would treat that valuation as unconfirmed.

  • We read one outlet's report in full. Check the company's own filing for exact terms before quoting any figure.

STORY 2 | BANKING

Citi and other banks say you own what your AI agent does

Banking. This week Citibank changed its online user agreement to say that customers who give third-party AI tools access to their accounts are responsible for what those tools do. TechCrunch reports that Citi goes a step further: if the AI tool leaks a customer's login details or gets hacked, the customer is responsible for what hackers do with the account. JPMorgan and Bank of America announced similar policies last month, PNC changed its terms on Wednesday, and Citizens emailed customers with the same warning.

The background is that AI agents can be given a bank login to track spending, give advice, or even pay bills. Banks say they will not reverse a transaction made by an agent, whether the mistake was intentional or not. TechCrunch notes that Citi's terms say AI agents the bank itself provides do not carry that extra liability, and it frames the moves as banks guarding a customer relationship as well as managing risk. Citi's own statement says it wants customers to understand the risks of sharing credentials.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

Before you hand any AI tool a login to money, email or an ad account, ask what happens if it goes wrong, and who pays. Read-only access is the safer choice. Brands will face the same question when AI agents start buying on behalf of customers, so this is a pattern worth watching for anyone in marketing. That is our take, not financial advice.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Make a small "AI access list" in any notes app or spreadsheet. For each AI tool your team uses, write what it can see, what it can change, and whether it can spend money. Turn off anything that can change or spend when you only need it to read.

Go deeper

  • TechCrunch points out that Anthropic's own personal finance tool, launched earlier this month, is billed as read-only and unable to move or invest money.

  • The article says markets briefly dipped at the idea of AI helping people dodge fees or low-interest accounts, which is part of why banks care.

  • It compares this to other industries where companies told AI agent makers to wait, naming car hailing and food service.

  • We read one outlet. We did not read Citi's agreement itself, only TechCrunch's description and the quoted statement.

Sources: TechCrunch (news report, one outlet read in full)

STORY 3 | SAFETY

Government testers got four popular chatbots to help plan attacks

Safety. The U.S. Government Accountability Office, the investigative arm of Congress, released a report on Wednesday, November 18. In covert tests in spring 2026, it prompted four of the most used generative AI platforms with two harmful scenarios. GAO says each platform produced feasible planning information, and each plan took under 12 hours to get. Two platforms gave information on building an explosive device meant for mass harm. The other two gave information on attacking the electric grid.

GAO says its first requests did trigger the platforms' safeguards, such as refusals, removed answers or suspended accounts. But it was able to get around them with common bypass techniques. It then checked the plans with research, surveillance and expert interviews and found them viable. The report anonymizes the platforms, so it does not say which company did what. The page we read says company officials generally stated they will keep improving safety, and it does not list recommendations.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU

Safeguards are a layer, not a guarantee, and regulators are now testing them directly. Expect this to feed into debates about AI rules, which can shape the AI tools marketing teams rely on every day. We are leaving out details of the tests on purpose. This is our reading of a government report, not a technical assessment.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY

Find the GAO page and write a two-sentence summary in your own words, then add one question the report leaves open, such as "which platforms did what?" Practicing short, sourced summaries helps when you write client reports and campaign briefs.

Go deeper

  • The report is numbered GAO-27-108814. It was dated October 16 and publicly released November 18, per GAO's page.

  • GAO says the platforms gave details such as specific targets, locations, timing and methods.

  • The four platforms are described as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok, shown anonymously as Platforms A through D. GAO does not link results to a name.

  • We read GAO's summary page, not the full PDF, so details beyond it are unchecked.

Sources: U.S. GAO report page (primary source). We read one source.

Gemini 4 Neon aims for strong results at a lower price and faster speed

Google | Gemini | Launched Nov 18

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on Wednesday. It says the model matched or beat its own Gemini 4 Carbon on overall quality at three times the speed. The price is $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with a one million token context window, which is roughly the length of a long book. Google lists it at 56.3% on Zapier's AutomationBench, first overall, and at 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, third overall.

Where to get it: the Gemini API in Google AI Studio, Gemini for business, Google Antigravity for paid Pro and Ultra subscribers, and the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers. The catch: every number here is Google's own report of outside tests, so wait for independent checks. Good for: seeing that "fast and cheap" is now the main race, not only "smartest." We have not tried it.

Sources: Google blog (primary source, vendor-reported). One source read.

Tip: get quick layout feedback on a campaign graphic

Upload a campaign graphic or social post image to any AI chat tool that accepts images and ask: "Read only, do not rewrite anything. Give me three layout changes, and tell me which one a customer would notice first."

How you might use it: it gives you a fast design review before a launch. Saying "read only" keeps the AI focused on feedback instead of rewriting your work.

Sources: A tip with no outside claim.

For choices and comparisons, tell the AI to work through the steps first and give its answer last. Then you can check the reasoning instead of trusting a one-line verdict.

WEAK

Which of these two ad ideas is better?

STRONG. COPY AND PASTE THIS

I need to choose between two ideas: [describe the decision, for example which ad concept to present].

Option A: [paste]

Option B: [paste]

What matters most: [2 or 3 criteria, for example clear message, fits the audience, easy to produce]

Work through this step by step before you answer:

1. Restate the decision in one sentence.

2. Score each option from 1 to 5 on each criterion, with one sentence of reasoning per score.

3. Name the biggest risk of each option.

4. Only then give your recommendation in two sentences.

Put your work under "Steps" and your pick under "Answer". If anything I gave you is unclear, ask me before you start.

Why the strong one works

  • You see the thinking. Each score comes with a reason, so you can spot a weak one and push back.

  • Answer comes last. The AI works the problem first, so the recommendation rests on the steps.

  • Your criteria lead. Listing what matters stops it from judging by its own taste.

  • Gaps get flagged. The last line invites a question before it guesses.

CLIP AND TRY TODAY. 5 MINUTES

Pick a real choice, such as which of two social post drafts to publish first. Run the prompt, then find the one score you disagree with and ask the AI to defend or change it. About 5 minutes.
